# Discovery to decision

Start with the user signal and its source. Write the smallest hypothesis that explains it, define an experiment with a falsifiable outcome, and record the decision after the evidence arrives. Link the proposal to the original signal and state what would change your mind.

## Separate the stages

1. **Signal:** what did a person do, say, or struggle with?
2. **Interpretation:** what might explain the signal, and what remains uncertain?
3. **Experiment:** what can we test within a bounded time and scope?
4. **Decision:** what did the evidence support, and what did it not answer?
5. **Knowledge:** which durable document should carry the lesson and owner?

Agents are useful for clustering repeated feedback and finding comparable decisions. They must preserve source links and mark interpretation as interpretation; a polished summary without provenance is not product knowledge.

When evidence invalidates an old decision, create a new proposal linked to both the old record and the new signal. History should show the evolution instead of pretending the first answer never existed.
