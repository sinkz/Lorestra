# Decision-making norms

Classify a decision as reversible, costly to reverse, or effectively permanent. For reversible decisions, prefer a bounded experiment with a review date. For costly decisions, require source links, dissent, and an explicit owner. For permanent decisions, record alternatives and governance approval.

## A decision is complete when

- the question and context are clear;
- the chosen option and rejected alternatives are named;
- dissent and residual risk are visible;
- an owner accepts the follow-up burden;
- a metric, event, or date can trigger reconsideration.

A decision is not complete until the next reader can tell what happened, why, and what evidence would invalidate it. Agents can make comparison and retrieval easier; they do not quietly close an unresolved disagreement.
